Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 19, 2026

Driftbottle is a location-based app where you drop and find virtual "message bottles" at real places. This policy explains what data we collect, how we use it, who can see it, how long we keep it, and who we share it with.

This is a plain-language summary, not a substitute for legal advice. If you have questions about your rights under a specific law (GDPR, CCPA, etc.), contact us at the address below.

Who operates Driftbottle

Driftbottle is operated by Andrea Bernal ("we", "us"), the party responsible for the data described here. You can reach us at support@cipher.haus.

Information we collect

  • Account information: username, email address, and password (stored as a salted hash — we never store or can see your actual password), plus when you accepted our Terms and Privacy Policy at sign-up. If you turn on two-factor authentication, we store your authenticator secret encrypted, and only hashed copies of your recovery codes.
  • Your birth month and year, at sign-up only: sign-up asks for the month and year you were born, only to check you're old enough (see Children's privacy). We don't store them.
  • Precise location: the app uses your device's precise GPS position to show you the bottles you can pick up where you are, to check you're close enough to pick one up, and to leave a bottle where you are when you drop one. Your position is sent to our server only with the request that needs it, used to answer that request, and then discarded: we don't store your location, and it isn't written to our logs. The one exception is where bottles are — and even then not your exact spot: a bottle you drop, throw back or leave with "Drop here" is kept at an approximate spot, a point within 40 metres of where you left it, and a bottle set to drift records the path it travels from there. Bottles you leave around the same place are all kept the same distance and direction from where you stood, so leaving many there doesn't give away the spot either. Location is core to how the app works and can't be turned off without losing that functionality.
  • Messages and other content you create: the messages you put in bottles — voice recordings and text messages — plus photos or drawings used as bottle labels, and bottle names. Your device writes the date and time into each recording; we remove it before the recording is stored.
  • Optional profile info: a display name ("bottler name") and any social media handles or links you choose to add.
  • Activity: which bottles you've dropped, found, saved, recycled or reported, users you've blocked, your coin balance and bottle inventory, and who referred you (if anyone).
  • Server logs: like most online services, our server logs each request it receives: the time, the part of the app requested, and your IP address. These logs do not include your location, messages or passwords.
  • Crash reports: if the app crashes or hits an error, we receive a report containing the error details and device/OS information, via our error-monitoring provider (Sentry). Crash reports don't include your location.
  • Emails you send us, if you write to support: your email address and what you write.

We do not use advertising trackers, and we do not sell your data.

What other users can see

Driftbottle is a social app, so some of what you create is visible to others:

  • Bottles you drop: players near a bottle you dropped see only that there's a bottle they can pick up, with its name and its size — not what's in it, who dropped it, or when. Whoever picks it up sees what's inside (the recording or text message, the label, and any social links you chose to share on it) and who it's from: every bottle shows its dropper's bottler name, or their username if they haven't set one. Your name isn't shown to anyone before a bottle is picked up, and no other player ever sees when you dropped it.
  • Bottles you pick up: when you pick up a bottle, its owner only sees that it was found — not who found it or where. The app doesn't show them when, either, though an owner who checks often could notice roughly when it happened. The app tells you this before you pick one up. Only you see when you found it.
  • Labels and keepsakes: if you label a bottle you found, whoever picks it up after you sees that label, but not who drew it. The bottle's owner never sees it at all — not the picture, and not that there is one — because a label appearing on their list the moment you drew it would tell them someone had just handled their bottle. A player holding one of your bottles can keep its label as a keepsake, along with the bottler name (or username) and social links the bottle showed; a player who recycles one of your labeled bottles, or picks up a labeled empty one, keeps a labeled bottle with that label in their inventory. You see the labels you put on your own bottles yourself, as you always did.

Never shown to other users: your email address, your password, any location, and when you dropped a bottle. Other players never see coordinates, a map, or how far away a bottle is — not for a bottle you dropped, not where you picked one up, and not where you are. The app only ever tells a player that a bottle is within reach. Driftbottle staff can see where bottles are, and when they were dropped, for moderation (see Moderation).

What the game itself gives away

Driftbottle is played at real places, so even without coordinates, some things can be worked out from it:

  • To see or pick up a bottle, you have to be within its pickup radius (50 to 100 metres) of where it's kept, which is up to 40 metres from where it was dropped. So someone who picks up a bottle you dropped, and sees your name, can tell you were within about 140 metres of where they are at some point (unless it has drifted, or been thrown back, since). The app doesn't tell them when — but a bottle can be found as soon as it's dropped, so someone who finds yours soon after you left it could guess you'd been there recently.
  • The owner of a bottle knows roughly where they left it, so when they see it's been found, they know someone was near there at some point since — though not who. The app doesn't show them when, but if they check often, they could tell roughly when it changed, and if they're nearby, they can see it's no longer there to pick up. A label you draw on their bottle doesn't tell them anything: they never see it.
  • A bottle you throw back, or leave with "Drop here", is left near where you are, and whoever picks it up next can tell someone left it there (but not who, or when).
  • The app can only go by where a device says it is. Someone who fakes their location (with a modified app, or a faked GPS position) could see and pick up bottles in places they aren't, and by trying many places, could work out roughly where a bottle is kept — but that's never the exact spot it was dropped on, only somewhere within 40 metres of it. Finding many bottles you left around the same place doesn't narrow it down, as they're all kept the same distance and direction from where you stood — though anyone who already knows the precise spot you left one of them on could work out where you left the others around there.

So don't drop bottles near your home, school or anywhere you don't want other players to know you spend time, and think about that before picking one up, too.

How we use your information

  • To operate the core gameplay: showing you the bottles you can pick up where you are, checking you're close enough to pick one up, leaving a bottle near where you drop it, and moving drifting bottles.
  • To check, when you sign up, that you're old enough to use Driftbottle.
  • To send you sign-in links, password resets, and verification codes by email.
  • To keep the service safe: rate limiting, detecting abuse, and investigating and acting on reports.
  • To diagnose and fix bugs or crashes.
  • To answer you when you contact us.

Moderation

To enforce our Terms, Driftbottle staff can view any bottle's content (including bottles nobody has picked up yet), its location, and the accounts involved, and can remove content or restrict accounts. Staff actions like these are recorded in an internal audit log. Reports you file are shared with staff, not with the person you reported.

When a bottle or a label is reported, we keep a copy of what was reported for our moderators: the recording, text message or picture, the bottle's name, and the username and email address of the account that posted it (and of whoever drew its label). For a reported bottle — not a label — the copy also keeps where it was dropped (the approximate spot it was kept at, as described under Precise location) and when. It keeps nothing about the person who reported it: not where they were, not where the bottle was when they reported it, and not when they picked it up. (To pick a bottle up you have to be within its pickup radius, so someone who reported a bottle they found was once within about 100 metres of where it was kept — unless it had drifted or been thrown back — but the copy doesn't say when.) The copy is kept even if the bottle, the label or the account is deleted afterwards, so that we can act on the report and, for child sexual abuse material, report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) and other authorities as the law requires. It's deleted automatically one year after the report — or, if we've reported it to NCMEC or the authorities have asked us to keep it, one year after that. See our Child Safety Standards.

Third parties we share data with

We use a small number of service providers to run the app. Each only receives the data it needs to do its job:

  • Render — hosts our server and database (all data listed above), including server logs and database backups.
  • Cloudflare — sits in front of our server (it sees your IP address and the requests you make, including the position the app sends with them, on their way through) and stores voice recordings and label images (Cloudflare R2).
  • Resend — sends sign-in, verification, and account emails (your email address and the email content), and our moderators' alerts when something is reported. An alert carries only the reason chosen and reference numbers for the report and the bottle — not what was reported, who filed it, or any note they added.
  • Mailgun — receives email sent to our cipher.haus addresses, including support@cipher.haus, and passes it on to our support mailbox (your email address and what you write).
  • Sentry — receives crash/error reports as described above.
  • Expo — delivers app updates. When the app checks for an update, Expo receives a random identifier for that installation of the app (not linked to your account, and reset if you reinstall), your device's platform and app version, and your IP address.
  • Open-Meteo — provides weather data for drifting bottles. It only receives rounded coordinates (to within about 50 km) of where drifting bottles are, never anything about you.

Each of these providers is bound by its agreement with us to protect your data at least as well as this policy describes, and to use it only to provide its service to us.

We don't share your data with anyone else, and we don't sell it. We may disclose information if required by law — including reporting child sexual abuse material to NCMEC, as described under Moderation.

Data retention

  • Your account and bottles are kept until you delete your account. Bottles you recycle are hidden from everyone else but stay in our database until then.
  • Drift paths are kept for up to 180 days, or 30 days once the bottle has been picked up or recycled; each bottle's original drop location is kept for as long as the bottle exists.
  • Your location isn't kept at all (see Precise location). Where your bottles are — an approximate spot near where you left each one — is kept as long as the bottles are.
  • Sign-in sessions, email links, and verification codes are deleted automatically once they expire, and records of recent sign-in attempts (kept to stop password guessing, and which can include your email address) within a few days.
  • Reported content: the copy we keep of something that was reported is deleted one year after the report, or one year after we reported it to NCMEC or the authorities asked us to keep it (see Moderation).
  • Server logs are kept by our hosting provider, Render, for no more than 30 days.
  • Crash reports are deleted by Sentry automatically within 90 days.
  • Database backups: our hosting provider, Render, keeps backups of our database for up to 7 days, so anything deleted from Driftbottle can remain in those backups for up to 7 days before they expire. We only use them to recover from a failure.
  • Emails we send (sign-in links, codes and account notices to you, and report alerts to our moderators) are kept by our email provider, Resend, for up to 30 days.
  • Emails you send us pass through Mailgun, which keeps records of them for up to 30 days. In our support mailbox we keep them only as long as we need them to handle your request and keep a record of it.
  • After you delete your account, we keep only what we need as a record: reports you filed or that concern your content, our internal log of the actions staff took on them and on your account (if we delete your account for you, including when you ask by email, that entry notes its username), records of any purchases (for accounting and refund handling), and anything the law requires us to preserve. These records aren't deleted on a fixed schedule; we keep them only as long as we need them for safety, legal or accounting reasons. The copy of anything of yours that was reported is kept for the year described above. Copies of your labels that other players already kept stay with them (see Deleting your account).

Deleting your account

You can delete your account at any time in the app: tap the account icon at the top right, then Account › Danger Zone › Delete Account, and confirm with your password. If you can't use the app, email us at support@cipher.haus from your account's email address and we'll delete it within 30 days, once we've confirmed the account is yours.

Deleting your account immediately deletes your account details and everything that's only yours: the bottles you dropped (including their recordings, text messages and labels), your keepsakes and inventory, and the labels you drew on other players' bottles. The recordings and pictures are deleted from our storage at the same time; if our storage provider can't be reached at that moment, we try again every day until they're gone. Bottles from other people that you were still holding go back into the world for someone else to find.

What stays: we don't remove copies of your labels that other players already kept. A keepsake keeps the label together with the bottler name (or username) and social links your bottle showed at the time; a labeled bottle in their inventory keeps the label (and so does any bottle they've dropped with it since). These stay with them until they delete them. The owner of a bottle you found and recycled still sees that it was found (never who found it). A copy of anything of yours that was reported is kept for a year, as described under Moderation, and our database backups expire within 7 days.

Delete your Driftbottle account has the full steps and everything that's deleted and kept.

Your choices and rights

  • You can delete your account at any time — see Deleting your account.
  • You can block another user from a bottle of theirs you found: neither of you will see the other's bottles after that. You can also block whoever drew a label you were shown: they won't see your bottles after that, and that label is hidden from you — but you may still come across their bottles, because we never tell you who drew a label, and hiding their bottles would. We don't tell anyone they've been blocked, though they may notice that they no longer come across your bottles. You can unblock anyone by tapping the account icon at the top right, then Blocked Bottlers.
  • You can request a copy of your data, correction of inaccurate data, or deletion by emailing us at the address below. We'll respond within 30 days.

Children's privacy

You must be 13 or older to use Driftbottle. Sign-up asks for the month and year you were born and doesn't let anyone younger than 13 create an account. We don't keep what you enter, and we don't keep an account, an email address or anything else you entered for someone it turns away. The app itself remembers on that device, for at least 30 days, that sign-up was turned down (not the month or year entered), so a different date can't simply be tried instead. Driftbottle isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. If we learn that someone under 13 has an account, we delete it and its data; if you believe a child under 13 is using Driftbottle, contact us.

Teenagers can use Driftbottle. If you're under 18, remember that other players are strangers. Whoever picks up a bottle you drop sees your bottler name (or your username), and so can tell you were somewhere near where they found it at some point (they don't see when you dropped it, and a bottle's owner only sees that it was found, not who found it). Choose a username and bottler name that aren't your real name, don't put your name, school, address or other personal details in a bottle or a label, don't drop bottles near your home or school, and talk to a parent or guardian if anything on Driftbottle worries you. Our Child Safety Standards explain how we prevent and respond to child sexual abuse and exploitation.

Security

Passwords are hashed (never stored in plain text), sensitive account secrets are encrypted at rest, all traffic to our servers is encrypted in transit (HTTPS), and recordings and pictures are only served through short-lived signed links.
